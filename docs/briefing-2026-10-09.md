# Briefing: detecting impactful expert load imbalance

Status as of 2026-10-07 (evening), for the 2026-10-09 update. Figures are in
[figures/](figures/) and are regenerated by `scripts/plot_findings.py`. The
details behind every number are in [status.md](status.md) and
[router-bias-findings.md](router-bias-findings.md).

## The message in one line

On this stack, realistic expert load imbalance costs nothing measurable. Strong
imbalance does cost latency, and the cost starts about 1.4x busiest-rank load
on Mixtral and between 2.3x and 3.5x on DeepSeek. Natural traffic peaks at 1.25x,
so a detector of *impactful* imbalance has to resolve the 1.4x and up band.

Setup: DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat (64 experts, top-6) and Mixtral 8x7B (8 experts,
top-2), 4-way expert parallelism on 4x Ascend 910B3, vLLM Ascend, 100-token
requests, 3 repeats per point in shuffled order.

## Suggested slides

1. **Question and setup.** What does expert imbalance cost a MoE deployment on
   Ascend, and how much imbalance has to be present before it matters?
2. **Realistic imbalance is free, and why** (results from 2026-10-02, no figure
   yet). Two mechanisms compose:
   - Concentrating tokens makes the fused-MoE GEMM cheaper per call, so ~20%
     token imbalance becomes ~2.6% time imbalance.
   - That 2.6% is buried in collective sync. About 95% of collective time is
     ranks waiting, paced by a rank that differs from run to run.
3. **The instrument: graded router bias** (`calibration.png`). A logit offset on
   one rank's experts takes the busiest rank from 1.14x to 3.45x while every
   expert stays live. Hollow points collapse routing and are excluded.
4. **Strong skew costs latency** (`dose_response.png`):
   - Mixtral: linear, +12% TPOT per +1x of load (t = 52), 165.5 to 209.8 ms,
     and it reproduces in graph mode.
   - DeepSeek: large and real by 3.2-3.5x; the shape below that is unresolved,
     since two eager sweeps disagree at 2.34x (+1.0% against +6.5%).
   - Graph mode shows the cost on both models with the instrument controlled.
5. **The key slide: impact map** (`impact_map.png`). Cost and natural traffic on
   one load axis:
   - Natural traffic, even a single prompt at p99, peaks at 1.25x. Topic-shifted
     windows reach 1.10-1.16x.
   - The first cost beyond the ±2.2% run-to-run drift is +3.3% at 1.38x
     (Mixtral).
6. **Detection: it is cheap, and it localises** (`detection.png`,
   [detection-findings.md](detection-findings.md)). 8 requests, ~1% of routing
   counted, every costly level caught. Window length buys more than counting
   more: one request at 100% is worse than 8 at 1.3%, because a single prompt is
   itself skewed. Per-layer flagging finds a skew the model-wide mean hides.
7. **Methods lessons** (`instrument_cost.png`):
   - The measurement plugin was missing from the balanced arm and cost 10 ms per
     token on DeepSeek. That inflated the first DeepSeek result by 43%.
   - Three apparent effects dissolved once repeated. Repeats are the only noise
     floor.
   - A neighbouring job on the node costs ~3% TPOT.
8. **Open questions and next steps** (below).

**On DeepSeek's threshold, be careful.** The morning sweep read flat to 2.34x
and the evening one +6.5% there, on a quiet node, with repeat spreads of 2-5 ms
and 9-14 ms respectively for the same configuration. Say that the cost is large
by 3.2x and absent at natural load, and that where it begins is being
replicated. The impact map draws both sweeps, so the slide shows the
disagreement rather than hiding it.

## Questions to expect

- **Why is DeepSeek a threshold and Mixtral a slope?** Unknown. The mechanism is
  not established on either model, and two per-step accounts were retracted.
  Fact we can state: a full-batch decode step is the same length at every eager
  offset on both models, so the cost sits in other kinds of step.
- **Is this just eager mode?** No. The graph-mode sweeps were re-run on
  2026-10-07 with the instrument in every arm and the cost survives (+22.6 ms
  per +1x on DeepSeek, t = 6.7). Caveat: their repeat spread is largest at the
  *low* levels, which fits frontend starvation - a fast engine the client cannot
  feed measures the client - so quote the slope, not the per-level numbers.
- **Was the node quiet?** No. An unrelated job ran on NPUs 4-7 during the
  controlled sweep. It does not line up with level, and the endpoints match a
  quiet-node control within 1.5%.
- **Is the load axis fair to natural traffic?** It is the busiest rank per MoE
  layer, averaged over layers. The coarser summary statistic used earlier lets
  the hot rank cancel across layers and reads 1.02x where this reads 1.14x.
- **What about wider expert parallelism?** It is the one lever that raised the
  straggler: 1.04x at 2 ranks, 1.13x at 4 and 1.31x at 8, extrapolating to ~1.6x
  at 16. That would approach the impactful band, so it is the main reason the
  null might not hold at scale.
- **Can we detect it?** Yes, and cheaply. A screen on busiest-rank load, counting
  1.2-1.5% of token-expert assignments over a window of 8 requests, flags every
  skew level that costs latency on both models at a 1% false-alarm rate
  (`detection.png`). It also localises: with the skew confined to 6 of 26 layers
  on rank 2, where the model-wide mean reads 1.40x, per-layer flagging recovers
  exactly those 6 layers and names the rank. Separately, router-drift detection
  catches gross router failure (AUC 1.0) but is blind below 10-20% misrouting.
